Version 1.3. Effective 7 October 2026.

This notice explains what personal data meterless collects, why, who sees it, where it is kept, for how long, and what you can do about it. It is written to be read. Where it links to a setting, the setting does what the notice says.

## 2. What we do not do

- We do not read, index, publish or share what you make or store in your studio. The one exception is an abuse review under section 3, which happens only on a specific reason and goes no further than needed.
- We do not use anything you make or store to train any model, ours or anyone else's.
- We do not sell personal data, and we do not share it for advertising.
- We do not use analytics or advertising cookies, session replay, or keystroke capture.
- We do not buy data about you from third parties.
- We do not pass your name, email address or account details to the data-centre supplier whose machine runs your studio.

## 3. What we collect, and why

**When you join the waitlist.** Your email address, the time you signed up, and where the signup came from. We use it to send you the confirmation you asked for and, when the founding cohort opens, an invitation. Basis: your consent, which you can withdraw at any time by replying to any of our emails or by writing to privacy@meterless.com. We do not store your IP address.

**When you open an account.** Your email address, the country you tell us you are in, your sign-in tokens, your plan and hours balance, and records of your sessions (which studio, when opened, paused, resumed and closed). If you sign in with Google, Google confirms your email address, your name and your Google account identifier to us, and nothing else. Google handles the sign-in under its own privacy policy. We give Google nothing about your use of meterless. We use these to run your account, to place your studio on infrastructure appropriate to your country (section 6), and to apply the make-good. Basis: performance of our contract with you; legal obligation for the records that tax and sanctions law require us to keep.

**When you pay.** Your name and billing address as you enter them at checkout, the country evidence used for tax (your billing address and the country your card was issued in), your subscription and invoice records, and the last four digits and brand of your card as our payment processor shows them to us. Your card number never reaches us; it is entered on Stripe's checkout page and held by Stripe. We use these to take payment, to calculate VAT or sales tax where the law requires, to issue invoices, and to handle renewals, cancellations, withdrawals and refunds. Basis: contract; legal obligation for tax and accounting.

**Sanctions screening.** Your name and country are checked against the sanctions lists of the United Nations, the United States, the European Union and the United Kingdom at signup and afterwards, and the result is kept. Our data-centre suppliers require this of us and the law requires it in any case. If a match closes your account, you can ask a person to review the decision at privacy@meterless.com. Basis: legal obligation.

**When you visit the site or use the app.** GitHub, which hosts the site, and Cloudflare, which fronts the app, each process your IP address and browser details to deliver the pages and protect against attacks, and keep their own logs; Cloudflare does so under its data-processing terms with us. We do not receive or store those logs. Our own application logs record what happened (which page, when, which account) and contain no visitor IP addresses. We use the country of your billing address, not your IP address, to apply the country rules in the Terms. When your browser connects to your studio, the connection passes through our edge, which removes your IP address before anything reaches the data-centre machine your studio runs on. Basis: our legitimate interest in running a secure service; you may object (section 8).

**When you write to us.** Your name, email address and what you write, at any of our addresses. We use it to answer you. Basis: contract, legal obligation (for rights requests), or legitimate interest.

**If we review your studio for abuse.** We do not monitor your studio. We review only when we have a specific reason to believe the Acceptable Use Policy or the law has been broken. A review may look at activity, inputs, outputs and stored files, by automated checks, a person, or both, and goes no further than needed. We keep a record of what we reviewed, what we found and what we did, and we tell you. Basis: legitimate interest in enforcing the Acceptable Use Policy and protecting others; legal obligation where the law requires us to act.

## 6. Where your studio runs, and international transfers

We are in the United Arab Emirates and our providers are in the European Union, the United Kingdom, Norway, Canada and the United States. Personal data can therefore leave the country you are in. Because we are established in the UAE, the account and billing data you give us is processed there; for customers in the EEA, the UK and Switzerland that transfer is necessary to perform our contract with you, and where a further safeguard is needed we use the Standard Contractual Clauses with our providers as set out below.

**Your studio.** If your account country is in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland, your studio runs on data-centre capacity inside the EEA or the UK whenever European capacity is available. When it is not, your studio may run in Canada or the United States, only on a supplier under a data-processing contract with us and, for the United States, the Standard Contractual Clauses; Canada has an adequacy decision. Your saved state is stored in EU-jurisdiction storage whichever country the studio ran in. The connection between your browser and your studio passes through our edge server in the region of the machine, and never through our control plane. If your account country is elsewhere, your studio may run on any of our capacity, including in the United States, and your saved state is stored in the United States.

**Everything else.** Where personal data goes to a provider outside the EEA, the UK or Switzerland, the provider's contract with us includes the European Commission's Standard Contractual Clauses and the UK Addendum to them. These are the standard legal terms that bind a provider abroad to European-level protection. Canada has an adequacy decision, which means the EU and UK recognise its protection as equivalent, so compute in Canada needs no extra terms. Transfers between the UK and the EEA rely on the adequacy decisions in force in each direction. Swiss data relies on the Swiss recognition of EU adequacy and the Swiss version of the Standard Contractual Clauses.

You can ask us for a copy of the transfer safeguards for any provider at privacy@meterless.com.

## 7. Cookies

The marketing site sets no cookies. The app sets one session cookie to keep you signed in, and keeps one flag in your browser's session storage to remember that you have seen the cookie notice; that flag never leaves your browser. Stripe's checkout sets its own cookies on Stripe's pages. There are no analytics or advertising cookies. The Cookie Notice has the details.

## 8. Your rights

Wherever you are, you can:

- **see and export** your account data from the account page, and your studio contents from your studio;
- **correct** your email address and country on the account page;
- **delete your account** from the account page; your subscription is cancelled, your data is marked for deletion, you have 7 days to reverse it, and deletion completes within 30 days, except the records section 5 says we must keep;
- **object** to processing we base on legitimate interest, **restrict** processing, and **withdraw consent** where consent is the basis, by emailing privacy@meterless.com;
- **ask us anything** about your data at privacy@meterless.com.

If you are in the EEA, the UK or Switzerland, these are your rights under the GDPR, the UK GDPR and the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection, and you also have the right to portability of the data you gave us. If you are in a US state with a privacy law (California, Colorado, Connecticut, Virginia, Utah and others), you have the same rights of access, correction, deletion and portability. We do not sell or share personal data, so there is nothing to opt out of. If you are elsewhere, we give you the same rights in any case.

We answer requests within one month; if a request is complex we may take up to two further months and we tell you why. We may ask you to confirm your identity through your account before we act. We do not charge for requests unless they are repetitive or unfounded, in which case we tell you the cost first.

**Complaints.** If you are unhappy with how we handle your data, tell us first at privacy@meterless.com. You also have the right to complain to a supervisory authority: in the UK the Information Commissioner's Office (ico.org.uk); in the EU your national authority (list at edpb.europa.eu); in Switzerland the Federal Data Protection and Information Commissioner (edoeb.admin.ch). You may contact our representative DataRep instead of us for any of this.

## 9. Children

meterless is for people aged 18 and over. We do not knowingly collect data from anyone under 18. If you believe we have, tell us at privacy@meterless.com and we will delete it.

## 10. Changes

We will revise this notice when our processing changes. If a change reduces your rights or adds a new purpose, we tell you by email at least 30 days before it takes effect. The version and date are at the top. Past versions are available on request.
